Project 03 · Published

DFIG Wind-Energy Fault Diagnosis using Machine Learning

Machine-learning fault detection and comparative studies for grid-connected DFIG wind energy conversion systems. Two IEEE publications.

Overview

Two connected studies on fault analysis for grid-connected Wind Energy Conversion Systems (WECS) using machine learning: an initial fault-analysis study, followed by a comparative study of several ML models.

My contribution

  • Phase 1 (Nov 2023 – Jul 2024): worked on fault analysis in grid-connected WECS using machine-learning techniques, under the mentorship of a research scholar and a professor. Built simulations and models in MATLAB, and extracted and analysed large datasets through custom code.
  • Phase 2 (Aug 2024 – Aug 2025): modelled and analysed a 9 MW DFIG-based WECS to study its dynamic response under symmetrical and asymmetrical grid faults.
  • Collaborated with the CINTEL team on ML-based fault classification using Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, SVM and XGBoost.
  • Contributed to a comparative framework for data-driven fault analysis, exploring the role of machine learning in improving fault prediction and grid reliability.

Technical scope

  • Grid-tied doubly-fed induction generator (DFIG) wind systems.
  • Symmetrical and asymmetrical grid faults.
  • Four ML classifiers benchmarked in the comparative study.

Status & outcomes

  • Phase 1 was presented at ICSPCRE 2024, NIT Rourkela, and published in IEEE Xplore (26 Nov 2024).
  • Phase 2 was presented at IConSCEPT 2025, NIT Puducherry, and is published in IEEE Xplore (24 Mar 2026).
